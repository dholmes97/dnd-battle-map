# Project direction

- Prefer a lightweight hexagonal boundary: keep battle-map rules and deterministic decisions in framework-free shared modules with direct unit tests, while React, Worker/D1, and browser APIs remain adapters around that core.
- Treat cohesive, high-resolution full-scene maps as the only map-authoring workflow. Do not restore the retired generic terrain or fragment-based editor.
- Scene additions must be generated for and visually matched to their specific base map.
- Keep the battle map visually dominant: desktop layouts must fit the map within the browser viewport, with controls and sidebars scrolling independently when needed.
- Keep global map launchers in the compact top toolbar; do not duplicate creature-palette or map-workshop controls in the right sidebar.
- Keep per-participant undo and redo durable and server-authoritative. Present both as compact top-toolbar icons, with Cmd/Ctrl+Z for undo and Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+Z plus Ctrl+Y for redo; never intercept these shortcuts while editing a field.
- Keep auto-expiring pings and DM spotlights out of Undo/Redo, including when rebuilding stacks from legacy action rows; only durable drawings are reversible annotations. When a newer shared edit prevents undo or redo, identify the affected move, HP, initiative, effect, drawing, placement, or token edit instead of showing a generic shared-state conflict.
- Keep tactical toolbar modes active after each use, including Ping, Draw Line, Erase, and DM spotlights; participants explicitly choose another tool when finished.
- Suppress native browser drag ghosts for spell placement. The transparent, locally animated map preview is the only spell artwork that should follow the pointer.
- Use one aligned X glyph system for close, discard, and compact removal actions, with styling matched to meaning; use a trash glyph for destructive list-item deletion instead of an ambiguous X.
- Treat initiative as a shared turn window: matching monster packs receive one durable group initiative by default—even when the DM edits a member card or corrects initiative during combat—and a character plus their summons always share one turn. Show exactly one player turn-ending action: End Turn for a lone token or End Group Turn when multiple tokens share the slot. Members can act in any order; offer an explicit Split from group action for exceptions.
- Preserve every rapid turn-advance click: paint each advance immediately, but serialize its authoritative request so repeated DM advances skip the intended number of initiative slots instead of collapsing onto one stale server turn.
- Size the desktop map from its live CSS container; do not freeze its dimensions from an early JavaScript layout measurement, and explicitly stretch the desktop grid column so the base flex layout's centered content rule cannot shrink-wrap it.
- Make Map Workshop reuse the player map's shared Fit geometry: derive one square cell size from the live canvas container, center the complete scene with letterboxing as needed, and use the same geometry for drawing and pointer coordinates.
- Cache the rendered full-scene map by stable map-package content rather than response-object identity. Ordinary live encounter updates must not tear down and rebuild the large backing canvas or reload unchanged scene assets.
- Keep fog of war as a durable scenario map setting with three DM-selectable modes: off, shared DM-controlled polygon fog, and dynamic per-player vision. The DM always sees the full map; players receive server-filtered tokens and annotations even though their browser also receives bounded vision geometry for immediate local painting.
- Let the DM prepare shared-fog corners and dynamic walls, doors, and round blockers privately in Map Workshop. Start shared fog with corners plus a midpoint on every side, allow corners to be added and removed down to a three-point minimum, and keep its corners directly draggable during play. Dynamic blockers are free-angle rather than grid-snapped: selected walls and doors can be translated or reshaped from either endpoint, while selected round blockers can be moved from their interior and resized from their rim. Reveal the footprint of a round blocker once a player can see its surface, while keeping its shadow opaque so the tree, column, boulder, or statue remains identifiable. Dynamic doors must be openable and closable during play. Vision guides are DM-only, player fog masks are fully opaque, and guide edits must not force the large scene canvas to rebuild.
- Short-circuit unchanged live-state polls before loading encounter collections or calculating vision polygons. Bound fog geometry so dynamic sight remains responsive even on heavily annotated maps.
- Freeze dynamic player vision at the last server-authoritative geometry throughout token dragging and while a move is pending. Moving a local token preview must never reveal terrain; replace vision only when the authoritative move response arrives, and reveal nothing from a rejected move.
- Preserve the map navigation convention: the desktop viewport fills the entire available stage, 100% uses a centered cover view with square grid cells, the dedicated Fit control or zooming out from 100% enters a whole-map Fit view (the only letterboxed state), scroll zooms toward the pointer, and left-dragging empty map space reveals cropped edges while token dragging remains direct; do not require right-click.
- The DM must be able to press and immediately drag any visible token on the map without selecting its sidebar card first.
- Keep selection independent from movement authority: every participant may select and inspect any visible token directly on the map. A durable scenario-specific DM-only Strict movement switch controls only dragging—on limits players to their character and summons; off lets anyone drag any visible token—while ownership continues to govern HP, initiative, editing, and dismissal.
- Keep the compact top-toolbar UI Settings menu separated by scope: Your display contains browser-local grid visibility plus positively worded Colored token centers and Health rings toggles, both enabled by default, while a DM-only Encounter settings section contains shared controls such as Strict movement. Never leave Strict movement permanently visible in the sidebar. Local visual controls must redraw immediately without shared-state traffic; turning Colored token centers off reveals the terrain and removes the inner ownership ring while preserving HP, selection, and effect indicators unless Health rings are separately turned off.
- Token moves, placements, and deletions must never globally block the map while awaiting confirmation. Preserve optimistic per-token mutations, reconcile them with authoritative responses, and roll back only the rejected token action.
- Treat repeated token drags during one active turn as revisions of a single move: persist the first-drag origin, measure every later destination from it, replace rather than accumulate movement used, and clear the origin only when that initiative slot becomes active again or combat is reset.
- Apply that optimistic rule to every battle-map interaction: annotations, initiative, HP, effects, token details/visibility, combat controls, encounter state, and local undo/redo must render immediately for the initiating participant while pending reducers survive live refreshes; synchronously commit the initiating paint before starting the network request, reconcile to authoritative state, and roll back only rejected mutations.
- Make encounter-state controls self-explanatory and tactile: Pause explains that it preserves combat state, Reset requires explicit confirmation and states what it clears, and both show pressed plus pending feedback.
- Starting combat from setup may act immediately, but restarting active or paused combat always requires confirmation that round, turn order, movement, and completed-turn tracking will reset.
- Restart and Reset controls always explain their distinct effects on hover and keyboard focus: Restart returns to round 1 with current initiative; Reset exits combat to setup. Both preserve map, tokens, HP, effects, and initiative values.
- Distinguish deliberate DM pauses from synchronization safety states in map messaging; never describe a DM-paused encounter as waiting for shared state to become current.
- Keep in-game chat durable and scenario-scoped, outside Undo/Redo, with an Everyone channel plus server-filtered private DM-to-player threads only. Launch it from the compact top toolbar with unread badges; use a movable, minimizable bottom-corner panel on desktop and a compact bottom sheet on mobile.
- Treat DM image handouts as scenario-scoped chat attachments with the same Everyone/private visibility rules. Prepare bounded display and thumbnail assets in the browser, preferring WebP with a storage-efficient JPEG fallback for Safari, discard originals, store only metadata in D1 and derived bytes in R2, and require the participant session on every asset fetch; players may view only handouts actually sent to them. Default the handout viewer to a true whole-image Fit mode for portrait and landscape assets, with an explicit scrollable Actual size mode.
- Let the DM optionally Show immediately when sending an attached handout. Apply it only to newly received live messages for connected recipients, queue simultaneous handouts, reset the option after every send, and never change chat unread state merely because the image opened. Offline recipients receive an ordinary unread attachment on return.
- Keep the join surface limited to the four trusted fixed identities: Dan (Dar'eleth), Barry (Jelton), and Scott (Malichar) as players, plus Kevin as DM. Do not restore free-form name, role, or encounter-code fields unless explicitly requested.
- Treat Ember Keep as one scenario, not the application identity. Populate the join-screen scenario chooser from durable encounter records so prepared scenarios keep independent maps, tokens, combat state, and history.
- Scenario creation is a DM-only in-map workflow. Fresh scenarios copy the established player party at full health; duplication copies the current map and tokens. Both start in setup with combat, initiative, effects, movement, and history cleared, switch the DM directly into the new scenario, and immediately appear in the join chooser.
- Scenario renaming is a DM-only in-map workflow. Rename only the durable display name and keep the scenario code stable so its map, tokens, combat state, and history remain attached.
- Never leave the fixed-identity join surface disabled indefinitely: bound the join request, restore every button after failure, and show a clear retry message.
- Resolve ownership from stable identities rather than browser sessions: Dan owns Dar'eleth and his summons, Barry owns Jelton and his summons, Scott owns Malichar and his summons, and Kevin owns everything else; the DM may control every token. Never restore claim, reconnect, release, unclaimed, or expiring-ownership UI.
- Keep the creature library storage-backed and lazy: searchable metadata belongs in D1, original/thumbnail bytes belong in R2, palette results are paged, thumbnails load only when visible, and full token art loads only for placement previews or tokens on the active map.
- Show the creature palette to players as well as the DM. Every player placement is forcibly created as that player's summon, rooted to their fixed character, sharing that character's initiative, and immediately controlled by that player; only the DM may place unattached monsters or choose a different summoner.
- Treat persistent map spells as first-class shared entities, not annotations: players place them through the spell palette, ownership is rooted to their fixed character, movement is optimistic and server-authoritative, spell motion does not consume creature movement, and controllers may dismiss their own effects. Render animation locally from deterministic shared state so no animation frames cross the network.
- Keep spell placement one-shot: after one click or drag placement, clear the armed spell selection immediately. Creature palette placement remains repeatable.
- Keep generic circle and square spell areas available as fallbacks. Let a spell's controller or the DM resize every persistent spell footprint from 5 to 20 feet after placement while preserving each dedicated spell's established default size.
- Let every participant select persistent map spells and inspect their sidebar cards. Keep non-owner cards read-only; only the spell's controller and the DM may move or dismiss the effect.
- Drive attached token VFX from the durable card effect itself. In particular, adding or removing Bless must automatically add or remove one clearly visible but restrained orbiting gold mote with a brief occasional flare, without a second placement workflow; do not add a halo, aura, or dotted ring.
- Render Haste from its durable card effect as a discrete blue-white pulse that appears at a deterministic random clock position on the token perimeter, expands and fades completely, then reappears elsewhere after a deterministic 2–3 second interval. Do not rotate, draw a tail, or create a complete ring or constant aura. Attached effect VFX must animate even when no persistent spell object is present.
- Keep Magic Circle's visible artwork on its full 10-foot-radius perimeter with a generous clear interior; compensate for transparent source-image padding rather than sizing only the image box. Render one artwork layer only—never add a smaller rotated echo inside it. Hit-test against the visible ring and prioritize character/creature hits so occupants remain easy to select and move. Do not draw the generic dotted ownership or dashed selection circle over Magic Circle—the artwork is already its boundary.
- Keep native spell-palette drag imagery suppressed with a persistent transparent drag image and disable native dragging on spell artwork, so Moonbeam and Flaming Sphere show only the square, screen-blended map preview instead of a black rectangular palette ghost.
- Let Medium and larger creature artwork fill the complete circular token footprint in both normal and transparent-center display modes, while Small creature art is intentionally inset to 75% within its mechanically full 5-foot token. Keep Large, Huge, and Gargantuan token outlines especially restrained so the art remains visually dominant.
- Creature catalog records must carry placement-ready defaults: average HP, hit dice, AC, challenge rating, creature type, size, and separate walk/fly/swim/climb/burrow speeds. Placement initializes current and maximum HP from the catalog default.
- Backfill legacy catalog-backed monster HP only when both current and maximum HP are null; never overwrite deliberately customized values.
- Grow the production creature catalog through the secret-protected batch importer in groups of at most ten; never commit its bearer token or authorize imports through the selectable DM role.
- Keep live synchronization tests aligned with the four fixed identities and use disposable tokens with guaranteed cleanup; never restore tests for claims, reservations, or expiring ownership.
- Verify changes locally first. Publish the Sites project only when the user explicitly asks.
- Preserve server-authoritative shared state, durable D1 history, temporary browser drafts, and the public accountless trusted-group model.
